Ćevapi, patties and skewers: mincing, cutting and shaping

A good ćevap holds its shape without egg or bread: the meat is fatty enough, salted, well kneaded and rested overnight. A skewer is good when all the pieces are the same size.

In short

  1. Keep the meat and the mincer cold — 20–30 minutes in the freezer before mincing.
  2. For 1 kg mince: 16–18 g salt (about 3 teaspoons) and 80–100 ml cold water.
  3. Knead for 5 minutes until the mixture turns sticky and pulls on your hand.
  4. Rest the mixture in the fridge for at least 2 hours, ideally overnight.
  5. A ćevap is 25–30 g and 6–7 cm long; a patty 1 cm thick and wider than the bread.

Mincing

Cut the meat into 3–4 cm strips or cubes and remove sinews and membranes. If you mince it yourself, put it through twice: first the coarse plate (8 mm), then the fine one (4–5 mm). Warm meat smears instead of mincing and the fat separates, so keep everything cold. Without a mincer, the butcher will mince the piece you choose.

Salt draws proteins out of the meat and they bind it — that is why the mixture is kneaded until sticky, not just mixed. The water keeps the ćevap juicy. Eggs, breadcrumbs and bread are not needed; they make it soft like a meatball.

Resting overnight is not just tradition: in that time the salt reaches all of the meat, the mixture firms up and is easier to shape.

Shaping

Ćevapi

Wet your hands with cold water. Take a 25–30 g piece (the size of a walnut in its shell), roll it between your palms into a finger 6–7 cm long and about 2 cm thick. Lay them on a tray without touching. It is quicker with a piping bag without a nozzle or with a 2 cm one: pipe a long sausage and cut it with a knife.

Patties

Put a 150–250 g piece between two sheets of film and press it with your palm into a round about 1 cm thick and 15–20 cm across. Press a shallow dip in the middle with your fingers — a patty shrinks and puffs up in the middle as it cooks, and this keeps it flat. Make it a little wider than the bread, as it shrinks.

Stuffed patty

Make two thinner rounds, put cheese and kajmak on one, leaving a 2 cm border, cover with the other and press the edges well with your fingers. Turn it only once.

Skewers

  1. Cut the meat into 2.5–3 cm cubes, all the same size, so they cook evenly.
  2. Soak wooden skewers in water for 30 minutes so they do not burn; metal ones need nothing.
  3. Thread the meat closely but not packed — leave a millimetre between pieces so the heat gets through.
  4. Put onion and pepper cut to the same size between the meat.
  5. Cook softer, thinner pieces (chicken) on their own skewers, not with beef.
  6. Two parallel skewers through the same meat stop the pieces spinning when you turn them.

Pounding, boning and butterflying

Pounding: put the steak between two sheets of film and beat it with the flat of a mallet from the middle outwards until it is even everywhere. Do not pound it thin like an escalope — for the grill leave it 1.5–2 cm.

Boning a chicken thigh: cut along the bone on the inside, scrape the meat away from the bone with the knife and cut the tendons at the joint. You get a flat piece that cooks quickly and evenly.

Butterfly: cut a thick chicken breast horizontally almost through and open it like a book. It cooks twice as fast and does not dry out on the outside.

When something goes wrong

The ćevapi fall apart on the grate
The mixture was not kneaded enough, not rested, or the meat was warm. Knead longer, rest overnight and turn them only when they come away from the grate by themselves.
The patty puffed up like a ball
There was no dip in the middle or it was too thick. Make it thinner and press the middle.
Skewers are burnt outside and raw inside
The pieces are too big or the fire too hot. Cut to 2.5 cm and cook over medium coals, turning more often.

Storage and freezing

Keep shaped ćevapi and patties in the fridge for a day at most. You can freeze them on a tray, then bag them, for up to 3 months. Grill them after thawing overnight in the fridge.
