Curing and drying meat at home

Curing is salting meat: briefly, for a few hours, so chicken and pork stay juicy on the grill — or for weeks, for dried meat that is then smoked and hung. Long curing needs cold weather, exact amounts of salt and curing salt.

In short

  1. Short brine for the grill: 50 g salt per litre of water; chicken 1–4 hours, pork 4–12 hours.
  2. Dried meat: 25–30 g salt per kg meat (2.5–3 %), part or all of it curing salt.
  3. Cure the meat at 2–5 °C — in the fridge, or in a cold room in winter.
  4. Time: about 1 week for every 3 cm of thickness.
  5. After curing the meat is rinsed, dried, smoked and hung until it has lost a third of its weight.

A short brine before grilling

Chicken breast, chicken legs and pork loin stay noticeably juicier if they spend some time in salted water before grilling. The salt goes into the meat and holds on to the water as it cooks.

MeatBrineTime
Chicken breast, skewers50 g salt + 1 l water1–2 hours
Whole chicken, legs50 g salt + 1 l water4–12 hours
Pork loin, neck in one piece50 g salt + 1 l water6–12 hours
Trout, mackerel (before smoking)50–60 g salt + 1 l water1–2 hours

You can add bay leaves, peppercorns, garlic and a tablespoon of sugar to the brine. The meat must be fully covered, in the fridge. Afterwards rinse it, dry it well and do not salt it again — just season.

Curing for dried meat

Dried meat is made from whole, clean boneless pieces: beef round (topside, silverside), pork neck, loin or leg. Pieces should be no more than 5–6 cm thick, so salt and smoke reach the middle.

Curing salt

Curing salt is table salt with a little nitrite. It keeps the meat red and, more importantly, stops the bacteria that cause botulism growing in meat that is smoked and dried for a long time at low temperatures. For meat that is cold-smoked and dried for weeks it is a must; follow the instructions on the pack and do not mix it with ordinary salt by eye.

Dry cure

  1. Weigh the meat. For every 1 kg weigh out 25–30 g curing salt (or the mix given on the pack).
  2. Add 2–3 g sugar, crushed pepper, bay leaf, garlic if you like.
  3. Rub the salt in well on all sides, put the meat in a sealed bag or a non-porous dish and keep it at 2–5 °C.
  4. Every other day turn it and massage it through the bag; leave any juice it releases with the meat.
  5. Keep it about 1 week for every 3 cm of thickness (a 6 cm piece — 2 weeks).

Wet cure

Make a brine of 1 l water and 70–80 g curing salt (as the pack says), boil it with bay and peppercorns and cool completely. Lay the meat in a dish, pour over the brine to cover and weigh it down with a plate. Keep at 2–5 °C, about 1 day for each centimetre of thickness, and turn it every few days.

Rinsing, smoking and drying

  1. Rinse the meat in cold water and leave it an hour or two in clean water to even out the salt on the surface.
  2. Hang it for a day in a cold, airy place to dry on the outside.
  3. Cold-smoke it (below 25 °C) a few hours a day for 1–3 weeks — see the smoking guide.
  4. Dry it at 10–15 °C and moderate humidity until it has lost 30–35 % of its weight. Weigh it now and then.

When something goes wrong

The meat is too salty
The salt was measured by eye instead of weighed. Soak salty pieces in cold water for a few hours before smoking. Next time weigh both meat and salt.
Hard outside, soft and wet inside
It dried too fast in warm, dry air. The outside sealed and the water cannot get out. Dry it cooler and in more humid air.
White or green mould appeared
A little white mould on the surface can be wiped off with a cloth dipped in vinegar. Green or black mould, a sticky surface or a bad smell — throw the meat away.

Safety

Do long curing and drying only with weighed curing salt, fresh meat and in the cold — below 5 °C while salting, below 15 °C while drying. If you have no cold space, cure in the fridge and then hot-smoke the meat and eat it cooked, not dried.

Storage

Keep dried meat cool and dry, wrapped in paper or cloth, not plastic. Once cut, keep it in the fridge. Vacuum-packed it keeps for several months in the fridge.
