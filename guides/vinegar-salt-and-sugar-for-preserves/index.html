Vinegar, salt and sugar: ratios for preserves

Preserves are kept by acid (vinegar or lemon), salt or sugar. You can change the flavour, but you must not cut these three by eye — they are why a jar lasts until spring.

In short

  1. Brine for pickles and pickled peppers: 1 part 9 % vinegar to at most 2 parts water (5 % vinegar: half and half).
  2. Per litre of brine: 20 g salt and 50–70 g sugar, to taste.
  3. Whole-head sauerkraut: 50 g coarse salt per litre of water. Shredded sauerkraut: 20 g salt per kilogram of cabbage.
  4. Jam: at least 500 g sugar per 1 kg prepared fruit. With less sugar — a water bath, and the fridge once opened.
  5. Ajvar: about 100 ml of 9 % vinegar per 5 kg peppers. Tomato passata: 1 tablespoon lemon juice in every 0.5 l jar.

Why acid

Peppers, aubergine, carrot, cauliflower, cucumber and even tomatoes are not acidic enough to be safe on their own in a sealed jar. In such a jar, with no air, a bacterium can grow that makes a dangerous toxin (botulism) without changing the smell or taste. Acid prevents it — which is why the vinegar in pickles is never diluted more than stated, and why ajvar and tomato passata always get some acid.

The acidity is printed on the bottle. If the pickles are too sharp for you, add more sugar or rinse them before eating — do not cut the vinegar.

Coarse non-iodised salt without anti-caking agents is best for pickling. Iodised salt is not dangerous, but it can cloud the brine and darken vegetables. For sauerkraut, weigh the salt — too little and the cabbage softens and spoils, too much and it will not ferment.

Lemon juice

Tomato passata gets 1 tablespoon of lemon juice per 0.5 l jar (2 tablespoons per 1 l jar), or ¼ teaspoon citric acid. Bottled juice has the most even acidity; fresh lemons vary, so if you use them, do not skimp. You can hardly taste it, and a pinch of sugar softens it once the jar is opened.

When something goes wrong

The pickles are too sour
Do not cut the vinegar next time — add sugar to the brine, and rinse the pickles briefly in cold water before eating.
The pickle brine is cloudy
If the lid is pulled in and it smells normal, the usual cause is iodised salt or hard water. If the lid bulges, the brine foams or smells strange — throw the jar away without tasting.
The jam will not set
Cook it longer, testing on a cold plate every few minutes. Runny jam is not spoiled, it just spreads — use it for cakes.
The sauerkraut is soft and slimy
There was too little salt or it was too warm (above 22 °C). Slimy sauerkraut that smells bad is thrown away; next time weigh the salt and keep the barrel at 15–20 °C.
