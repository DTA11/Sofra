When a jar goes bad: the signs and what to do

Spoiled preserves are recognised with your eyes, ears and nose — never your tongue. If in doubt, throw the whole jar away: one jar of ajvar is not worth the risk.

In short

  1. Throw a jar away if the lid bulges, is loose or clicks, if it spurts when opened, if bubbles rise in it (unless it is sauerkraut), if it has mould or smells strange.
  2. Never taste a doubtful jar, not even the tip of a spoon, and do not give it to animals.
  3. Do not open a bulging lid near your face. Put the closed jar in a bag, tie it and put it in the bin.
  4. If the contents leaked, clean everything with bleach diluted with water (1 : 5) and wash your hands.
  5. If anyone who ate from a jar gets double vision, drooping eyelids or difficulty swallowing and speaking — call an ambulance at once.

Why you never taste it

The most dangerous kind of spoilage — botulism — need not be seen or smelt at all. The bacterium grows in a sealed, airless jar of vegetables that are not acidic enough: ajvar, tomato passata without lemon juice, vegetables in oil, beans or meat in jars. The toxin it makes is dangerous even in tiny amounts. That is why a doubtful jar is thrown away untasted, and why ajvar and tomato passata always get acid and the jars are boiled. It does not grow in jam with enough sugar, in properly made pickles or in sauerkraut.

How to throw a jar away safely

  1. Put the closed jar, lid on, into a plastic bag, tie it and put it into a second bag. Put it in the bin — not on the compost, not down the toilet, not to animals.
  2. If the jar is open or has leaked: put on gloves, soak up the contents with paper towels and put them in the bag with the jar.
  3. Wipe the surfaces (shelf, table, sink) with bleach diluted with water (1 part bleach to 5 parts water), leave for 30 minutes, then wash with water.
  4. Throw away sponges and cloths that touched it. Take off the gloves and wash your hands well with soap.

Mould on jam and plum butter

People used to scrape the mould off the top and eat the jam. Today the advice is to throw away the whole jar: mould sends threads deeper than you can see, and some kinds make toxins. In an opened jar in the fridge it means it stood too long or was served with a dirty spoon.

What is not a sign of spoilage

A white film on sauerkraut
Yeasts on the air — skim them off. The cabbage under the brine is fine.
Cloudy brine with sauerkraut
Normal with any fermentation.
A little white sediment at the bottom of pickles
Usually salt, spices and hard water. If the lid is pulled in and the smell and colour are normal — it is not dangerous.
Sugar crystals in jam or plum butter
The jam was cooked too long or is old. Not dangerous — warm it with a spoonful of water.
A darker top layer
Air in the jar. If the lid is pulled in and everything else is normal, it is not dangerous.

If someone falls ill

Signs of botulism usually start 12–36 hours after eating (sometimes later): double or blurred vision, drooping eyelids, slurred speech and difficulty swallowing, a dry mouth and muscle weakness that spreads. Call an ambulance at once and say what was eaten. Keep the jar and any leftovers closed, in a bag, for the doctors.

How to stop it happening again

The usual causes: the jar was not boiled in water or not for long enough, too little vinegar or lemon juice, a chipped rim, an old lid, a storage place that is too warm. It is all in the guide Jars for preserves: washing, sterilising and the water bath.
