Sauerkraut at home: whole heads in a barrel and shredded in jars

Cabbage sours itself: salt draws out the juice and good bacteria turn the sugar in the cabbage into acid. Your job is to keep the cabbage under brine at all times, get the salt right and keep the barrel at the right temperature.

In short

  1. Use late, hard, heavy heads — early summer cabbage is soft and goes limp in brine.
  2. Brine: 50 g coarse salt per litre of cold boiled water; plus a tablespoon of salt in each hollowed core.
  3. The heads must stay under at least 5 cm of brine, held down with a board and a stone.
  4. Keep the barrel at 15–20 °C and turn the brine every 2–3 days for the first three weeks.
  5. It is ready in 4–6 weeks; after that keep it cold (below 10 °C).

For a family of four who make sarma, baked sauerkraut and sauerkraut salad in winter, a 30-litre barrel is usually enough: count 3–4 heads per person for the whole winter.

Whole heads in a barrel, step by step

1. The container

The barrel or bucket must be food-grade (plastic marked for food, wood or glazed stoneware), clean and washed in hot water. A bucket with a tap at the bottom is most practical — the brine is turned through it. You also need a board that fits inside and a heavy clean stone or a container of water.

2. The heads

Remove the green outer leaves (keep a few good ones for the top). Cut a cone out of the core, 4–5 cm deep, and put a tablespoon of coarse salt in the hole.

3. Packing

Pack the heads tightly, holes up, filling gaps with small heads or halves. Add bay leaves, peppercorns and, if you like, a piece of horseradish or quince in between. Cover the top with the saved leaves.

4. Brine and weight

Make the brine from cold boiled water and salt and pour it over the heads. Put on the board and stone — the heads must be under at least 5 cm of brine and stay that way to the end. Cover the barrel with its lid or a clean cloth.

5. Temperature

15–20 °C is best. Colder and the cabbage sours slowly and stays bland; above 22 °C it softens and spoils easily.

6. Turning the brine

After 3–4 days start turning: run off 4–5 litres of brine through the tap (or a hose lowered to the bottom) and pour it back over the top. This evens out the salt and lets the gases escape. For the first 3 weeks turn it every 2–3 days, then once a week. If the brine drops, top up with new brine (50 g salt per litre).

7. When it is ready

In 4–6 weeks the leaves are sour, translucent and a nice yellow. Then move the barrel somewhere cold — a cellar, balcony or garage below 10 °C, but where it will not freeze. Take heads out with clean hands or tongs and put the board and stone straight back.

Shredded sauerkraut in jars

Shred the trimmed cabbage, weigh it and mix in 20 g salt per kilogram (and caraway if you like). Squeeze it with your hands until it gives off plenty of juice, then pack it hard into jars until the juice covers it, leaving 3–4 cm below the rim. Top with a cabbage leaf and something heavy (a small jar of water). Just rest the lid on — the gases must escape — and stand the jars on a plate. Keep them at 18–20 °C and press the cabbage down with a clean spoon every day. In 2–4 weeks it stops bubbling and is sour: then close the jars and put them in the fridge or a cold cellar.

What is normal and what is not

A white film on the surface of the brine
Normal — yeasts on the air. Skim it off when you turn the brine. The cabbage under the brine is fine.
Bubbles, foam and a sour smell in the first weeks
A sign that fermentation is working. That is why the brine is turned and the shredded cabbage pressed every day.
The brine is cloudy
Normal with sauerkraut. It only matters together with the other signs below.
The cabbage is soft, slimy, pink or smells rotten
There was too little salt, it was too warm or the cabbage was not under the brine. Throw those heads away; if the whole barrel is like that, throw it all away and wash the container well.
Furry green or black mould on the surface
Remove the mould and the top heads it touches; if it has got into the cabbage or keeps spreading, throw everything away. Prevent it by keeping the cabbage under brine at all times.
The cabbage is not souring
It is too cold. Move the barrel somewhere warmer (15–20 °C) and keep turning the brine.

Storage

Heads in a barrel last until spring if kept cold and under brine; once it warms up they soften. Shredded sauerkraut in closed jars keeps 4–6 months in the fridge. Sauerkraut can also be frozen in portions.
