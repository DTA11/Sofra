How to cook rice so it comes out fluffy

Rice turns sticky or burns mostly for three reasons: too much water, stirring, and too much heat. Measure the water, keep the lid on and let it rest.

In short

  1. Rinse the rice in cold water 2–3 times, until the water is nearly clear.
  2. For 1 cup of rice use 1½ cups of water and ½ teaspoon of salt.
  3. Bring to the boil, then turn the heat to the lowest setting and cover.
  4. Cook 12–15 minutes without stirring or lifting the lid.
  5. Take off the heat, leave covered 10 more minutes, then fluff with a fork.

How much water for how much rice

Measure rice and water with the same cup or glass, so the size of the cup doesn't matter. These ratios are for cooking with a lid, until the rice has absorbed all the water.

Packets often say 1 : 2. For plain white rice cooked under a lid that is usually too much water, and the rice comes out soft and sticky. Start with 1 : 1½, then adjust next time to your own pot and hob.

How much rice per person

Rice grows about two and a half to three times as it cooks. A 250 ml cup holds about 200 g of raw rice, a tablespoon about 15 g.

Step by step

1. Rinse the rice

Put the rice in a bowl, cover with cold water, swirl with your hand and drain. Repeat until the water is nearly clear. This washes off the surface starch that makes rice stick. Don't rinse rice for rice pudding or risotto — there the starch is what thickens the dish.

2. Toast it briefly, if you like

For a pilaf or a side with more flavour, fry the drained rice in a spoonful of oil or butter for 1–2 minutes, stirring, until the grains look glassy. A little finely chopped onion works well too.

3. Add water and salt

Pour in the measured water or stock and season: ½ teaspoon of salt per cup of rice. If you use a stock cube, use less salt or none.

4. Cook covered on the lowest heat

As soon as it boils, turn the heat to the lowest setting, on the smallest ring, and cover. Don't stir and don't lift the lid — the steam is what cooks the rice.

5. Rest

When the time in the table is up, take the pot off the ring and leave it covered for 10 minutes. If you like, put a tea towel between pot and lid to catch the extra steam. Finally fluff the rice with a fork, not a spoon.

The foolproof way: like pasta

If the ratio doesn't suit your pot, boil the rice in plenty of salted water like pasta for 11–13 minutes. Bite a grain, drain, and return it to the pot covered for 5 minutes. It can't burn or turn to mush.

When something goes wrong

The rice is sticky or mushy
Too much water, it was stirred, or it wasn't rinsed. Next time use less water and leave it alone while it cooks. Use this batch for rice pudding, stuffed peppers or soup.
It burnt on the bottom
The heat was too high or the pot is thin-bottomed. Don't scrape the bottom; move the top part into another dish. Next time use a heavy pot and the smallest ring.
The grains are hard in the middle
Too little water, or the lid was lifted. Add 2–3 tablespoons of boiling water, cover and keep on the lowest heat for another 5 minutes.
There is water left at the bottom
Cook uncovered for 2–3 more minutes until it evaporates, then cover and let it rest.
The rice tastes of nothing
Cook it in stock instead of water, toast it in butter first or add a bay leaf.

Leftover rice

Don't leave cooked rice at room temperature for hours. Once it has cooled, within an hour at most, put it in the fridge and eat it within a day. Reheat it only once, until it is piping hot all the way through. Yesterday's cold rice is perfect for fried rice or stuffed peppers.
