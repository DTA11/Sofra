Smoking meat and fish: hot and cold

With hot smoke the meat is smoked and cooked at the same time — ready to eat in a few hours. Cold smoke only adds flavour and dries: that is for cured meat that is then hung for weeks.

In short

  1. Hot smoking: 80–110 °C, the meat cooks; trout 1–1.5 hours, chicken 2–3 hours.
  2. Cold smoking: below 25 °C, a few hours a day for 1–3 weeks, only for cured meat.
  3. Wood: beech, oak, fruit woods (apple, plum, cherry); never conifers or treated wood.
  4. The smoke should be thin and bluish, not thick and white.
  5. Salt or brine the meat before smoking and let the surface dry.

The wood must be dry and free of flaking bark. Conifers and varnished, painted or treated wood give a bitter, harmful smoke.

Hot smoking in a lidded grill

  1. Keep the fish or meat 1–12 hours in a short brine (50 g salt per litre of water — see the curing guide), rinse and leave it an hour in the air so the surface dries.
  2. Light half the usual charcoal and bank it against one side of the grill.
  3. Put a handful of wood chips or sawdust, or a small piece of hardwood, on the coals. You can also put the chips in a foil tray with holes.
  4. Put the meat on the other side of the grate, close the lid and set the lid vent above the meat so the smoke passes over it.
  5. Keep it at 90–110 °C (lid thermometer or one at meat level) and add chips when the smoke stops.
  6. It is done at 74 °C in the middle for chicken, 60 °C for fish, 70 °C for pork.

Cold smoking

Only meat cured with curing salt is cold-smoked (see “Curing and drying meat”). The fire is apart from the meat chamber and the smoke reaches it through a pipe or channel several metres long, cooling on the way. The smokehouse is fired a few hours a day, mostly in winter and at night when it is cold, and in between the meat dries. For dried meat this takes 1–3 weeks, until it is dark and smells of smoke.

When something goes wrong

The meat is bitter and black
The smoke was too thick, the wood damp or it burned with too little air. Open the air supply, use dry wood and add it little by little.
The fish fell apart
It was not brined or dried enough before smoking, or the temperature was too high. Brine first, dry for an hour, then no more than 100 °C.
When cold smoking the outside of the meat cooked
The smoke was too warm. Move the fire further from the meat or smoke only when it is cold outside.

Safety

Cold-smoked meat is not cooked: it must be cured with curing salt and smoked in the cold, otherwise dangerous bacteria can grow in it. If in doubt, hot-smoke and eat the meat cooked. Keep the smokehouse away from the house and never smoke indoors.

Storage

Hot-smoked fish and meat keep 3–5 days in the fridge, wrapped. Cold-smoked dried meat keeps like any dried meat — cool and dry, or vacuum-packed in the fridge.
