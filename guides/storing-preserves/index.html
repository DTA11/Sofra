How to store preserves and how long they keep

A well-sealed jar does not spoil overnight, but light, warmth and damp slowly take away its colour, flavour and lid. A store for preserves should be dark, cool and dry.

In short

  1. Keep jars somewhere dark, cool and dry, at 5–15 °C — never above the cooker, by a radiator or in the sun.
  2. Closed preserves are best within a year; use them before the next season.
  3. An opened jar goes into the fridge — times are in the table.
  4. Always use a clean, dry spoon, and never put leftovers from the plate back in the jar.
  5. Label every jar with what it is and when it was made, and put older jars at the front.

Where to store them

Cellar or larder

The best place: dark, 5–15 °C, not damp. Stand the jars on shelves rather than stacking them — the weight can push up the lid of the jar below.

A flat without a cellar

A cupboard in the coolest room, away from the kitchen and radiators, or a closed box in a bedroom. Light bleaches ajvar and tomato passata, so keep jars in boxes or behind doors.

Balcony and garage

Fine for a barrel of sauerkraut until the frosts. Jars freeze and crack in frost, and food that has frozen and thawed changes texture.

Order in the pantry

Enter what you make into Sofra’s Pantry (the “Made — to the pantry” button) — the menu then uses it for sides and knows how many jars you have. New jars at the back, old ones at the front. In spring, note what is left: that tells you what to make less of and what more.

When something goes wrong

A jar froze
If it cracked or the lid was pushed off — throw it away. If it is whole and the lid is pulled in, it can be eaten, but the vegetables will be softer.
Preserves from last year
If the lid is pulled in and the colour, smell and look are normal, they can be eaten — just weaker in flavour. Use them first.
The lid is rusty
If the rust is only on the outside and the lid is pulled in, use the jar soon. If the rust is underneath, on the rim or touching the food — throw the jar away.
